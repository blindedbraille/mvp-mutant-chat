FILE 01 // CANADA

Weapon X // X-Man

Wolverine

Logan · James Howlett

First full appearance The Incredible Hulk #181 · 1974

“I'm the best there is at what I do.”— Logan

Origin // Field notes

Born in 19th-century Canada, Logan's mutation includes accelerated healing and retractable bone claws. The Weapon X program later bonded adamantium to his skeleton and erased much of his memory. His long, fragmented life eventually brings him into the X-Men, where a lone survivor becomes part of a team.

Creators // first full debut

Len Wein (writer); John Romita Sr. (character design); Herb Trimpe (artist on the full debut). The teaser appeared in The Incredible Hulk #180; #181 is the full debut.

Powers & skills

Accelerated healing, heightened senses, an extended lifespan, retractable claws, and exceptional close-quarters combat skill. Adamantium is the result of experimentation, not his original mutation.

Margin note

His first appearance is a two-part publication story: a final-panel teaser in The Incredible Hulk #180, followed by the full debut in #181. John Romita Sr. designed the character; Len Wein wrote the debut and Herb Trimpe drew it.

Start reading

  • Giant-Size X-Men #1 (1975)
  • Wolverine #1–4 (1982), by Chris Claremont and Frank Miller
  • “Weapon X,” Marvel Comics Presents #72–84 (1991)

FILE 02 // KENYA

Queen // X-Man

Storm

Ororo Munroe

First appearance Giant-Size X-Men #1 · 1975

A leader who can change the weather—and the room.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Ororo Munroe was born in New York and grew up in Cairo before journeying to Kenya. Her weather-manipulating mutant gift led some people to revere her as a goddess. Professor X recruits her to the new international X-Men team in 1975; she grows into one of its most trusted leaders.

Creators // team debut

Len Wein (writer) and Dave Cockrum (artist and character designer). Storm joined the new team in Giant-Size X-Men #1.

Powers & skills

She can influence weather patterns, summon wind, rain and lightning, alter temperature, and fly on currents she creates. Her control and judgment are as central to her strength as raw power.

Margin note

Artist Dave Cockrum combined ideas from two earlier designs of his—Black Cat and Typhoon—when developing Storm's look. Her signature white hair was part of the design from the start.

Start reading

  • Giant-Size X-Men #1 (1975)
  • “Lifedeath,” The Uncanny X-Men #186 (1984)
  • Storm (2024), by Murewa Ayodele and Lucas Werneck

FILE 03 // CLASSIFIED? SURE.

Mercenary // Occasionally helpful

Deadpool

Wade Wilson

First appearance The New Mutants #98 · 1990 (1991 cover date)

This file has been reviewed by its subject. He added three footnotes.— Unverified archivist

Origin // Field notes

Wade Wilson debuted as a masked mercenary sent after the New Mutants. Later stories connect his cancer treatment to the Weapon X program, which gave him a powerful healing factor and left him visibly scarred. His fourth-wall-breaking narration and erratic humor became part of his signature.

Creators // debut

Fabian Nicieza (writer) and Rob Liefeld (artist and co-creator). The New Mutants #98 was cover-dated February 1991 and went on sale in December 1990.

Powers & skills

Accelerated healing, high pain tolerance, expert sword and firearm use, and formidable improvisational combat skills. The healing factor does not make him invulnerable.

Margin note

Rob Liefeld created the visual design; writer Fabian Nicieza named him and established much of his wisecracking voice. The name “Deadpool” came from a pool about who would die, not from an elaborate super-science acronym.

Start reading

  • The New Mutants #98 (1991 cover date)
  • Deadpool: The Circle Chase #1–4 (1993)
  • Deadpool (1997) #1 onward, by Joe Kelly and Ed McGuinness

FILE 04 // PHOENIX

Founding X-Man // Phoenix

Marvel Girl

Jean Grey · Phoenix

First appearance The X-Men #1 · 1963

A founding student whose story became cosmic in scale.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Jean Grey was one of the original students recruited by Professor X and the first woman to join his X-Men. Initially known as Marvel Girl, her telekinetic abilities and later telepathy placed her at the center of the team's history. Her connection to the Phoenix Force drives one of the best-known X-Men sagas.

Creators // debut

Stan Lee (writer) and Jack Kirby (artist and co-plotter). Jean first appeared as Marvel Girl in The X-Men #1.

Powers & abilities

Powerful telepathy and telekinesis. Her relationship with the Phoenix Force has changed across different periods of Marvel continuity; it is a distinct cosmic story element, not a simple synonym for her mutant powers.

Margin note

In the earliest X-Men stories, Jean's codename was Marvel Girl. “Phoenix” became part of her identity years later, after the team's spaceflight story.

Start reading

  • The X-Men #1 (1963)
  • “The Phoenix Saga,” The Uncanny X-Men #101–108 (1976–77)
  • “The Dark Phoenix Saga,” The Uncanny X-Men #129–137 (1980)

FILE 05 // LAB NOTES

Founding X-Man // Scientist

Beast

Dr. Henry “Hank” McCoy

First appearance The X-Men #1 · 1963

A brilliant mind with the agility to match.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Hank McCoy was a founding X-Man whose extraordinary strength and agility appeared alongside a remarkable intellect. A gifted biochemist, he later experimented on himself to reverse a physical mutation; the procedure instead left him covered in blue fur. Across eras he has worked as a hero, teacher and scientist.

Creators // debut

Stan Lee (writer) and Jack Kirby (artist and co-plotter). Beast debuted with the original team in The X-Men #1.

Powers & abilities

Superhuman strength, speed, agility, balance and dexterity; enhanced senses; and a genius-level scientific intellect. The exact details of his physiology have shifted as his appearance changed over the decades.

Margin note

Hank's furry blue appearance was not how he began: he first appeared with a human-looking body and unusually large hands and feet. His transformation unfolded in later comics.

Start reading

  • The X-Men #1 (1963)
  • Hank's transformation in Amazing Adventures #11–17 (1972)
  • Astonishing X-Men (2004) #1 onward, by Joss Whedon and John Cassaday

FILE 06 // FUTURE FRONT

Soldier from tomorrow // X-Force

Cable

Nathan Christopher Charles Summers

First teaser The New Mutants #86 · 1990 First full appearance: #87

“Forty-eight. I sent another one.”— Cable, probably

Origin // Field notes

Cable was introduced as a grizzled future warrior and the new leader of the New Mutants. Later stories revealed him to be Nathan Summers, the son of Cyclops and Madelyne Pryor, sent into a future where he could survive a techno-organic virus. The character's backstory was deliberately revealed in pieces.

Creators // first full debut

Louise Simonson (writer) and Rob Liefeld (artist and character designer). Cable appears as a teaser in The New Mutants #86 and makes his first full appearance in #87, both published in 1990.

Powers & skills

Telepathy and telekinesis, commonly constrained by the effort required to contain his techno-organic infection. He is also a time traveler, strategist and heavily trained combatant, though his equipment and powers vary by era.

Margin note

Cable did not arrive with his complete family history. Readers first met the mysterious adult soldier before the books established his connection to the Summers family.

Start reading

  • The New Mutants #86–87 (1990)
  • “X-Cutioner's Song,” X-books crossover (1992)
  • Cable & Deadpool #1 onward (2004)

FILE 07 // FIELD COMMAND

Founding X-Man // Team leader

Cyclops

Scott Summers

First appearance The X-Men #1 · 1963

The plan is the plan. Even when the plan changes.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Scott Summers was the first student recruited by Professor X and became the original X-Men's field leader. His mutant optic blasts are powerful and continuous, so he relies on a specially engineered visor to control them. Discipline and tactical judgment become as recognizable as the red beam.

Creators // debut

Stan Lee (writer) and Jack Kirby (artist and co-plotter). Scott Summers was part of the original lineup in The X-Men #1.

Powers & abilities

His eyes emit concussive force beams. Ruby-quartz lenses and his visor regulate the blasts; they are not heat or laser vision. Scott is also a trained tactician and experienced team commander.

Margin note

Cyclops and Beast were both introduced in the very first issue, but Scott was chosen as the field leader of the original team.

Start reading

  • The X-Men #1 (1963)
  • Giant-Size X-Men #1 (1975)
  • “The Dark Phoenix Saga,” The Uncanny X-Men #129–137 (1980)

FILE 08 // CEREBRO

Founder // Telepath

Professor X

Charles Francis Xavier

First appearance The X-Men #1 · 1963

A school, a team, and a dream of coexistence.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Charles Xavier is a powerful telepath and the founder of the X-Men and Xavier's School for Gifted Youngsters. He gathers young mutants to help them understand their abilities and to defend people who fear them. His longstanding ideological debate with Magneto asks how mutants can survive in a hostile world.

Creators // debut

Stan Lee (writer) and Jack Kirby (artist and co-plotter). Professor X was introduced as the mentor of the original X-Men in issue #1.

Powers & abilities

Extensive telepathy, including mind-to-mind communication and mental perception. Cerebro amplifies and focuses his ability. His intelligence and teaching shaped generations of mutant heroes.

Margin note

In the original 1963 series, Xavier is introduced as the headmaster of the school and mentor of the first five X-Men. His long friendship and philosophical conflict with Magneto were developed later.

Start reading

  • The X-Men #1 (1963)
  • Marvel Graphic Novel #5: God Loves, Man Kills (1982)
  • X-Men: Deadly Genesis #1–6 (2005–06)

FILE 09 // POLARITY

Brotherhood // Sometimes ally

Magneto

Max Eisenhardt · also known as Erik Lehnsherr

First appearance The X-Men #1 · 1963

Same fear. A different answer to it.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Magneto is a mutant whose ability to control magnetism is matched by a history shaped by the Holocaust. His experiences underpin a conviction that mutants must protect themselves from human persecution. His methods put him at odds with Charles Xavier, although their relationship has shifted between enemy, ally and friend.

Creators // debut

Stan Lee (writer) and Jack Kirby (artist and co-plotter). Magneto was the first major adversary faced by the X-Men in The X-Men #1.

Powers & abilities

He can manipulate magnetic fields and metal, generate force fields, and influence electromagnetic phenomena. The extent of those powers varies with the story and the writer.

Margin note

His civilian identity has been revised in comics over time. This file gives the currently familiar Max Eisenhardt name alongside Erik Lehnsherr, another widely used identity.

Start reading

  • The X-Men #1 (1963)
  • Marvel Graphic Novel #5: God Loves, Man Kills (1982)
  • X-Men: Magneto Testament #1–5 (2008–09)

FILE 10 // BAMF

X-Man // Swashbuckler

Nightcrawler

Kurt Wagner

First appearance Giant-Size X-Men #1 · 1975

A teleporting acrobat with a showman's heart.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Kurt Wagner is a German mutant with blue skin, pointed ears, a prehensile tail and the ability to teleport. Dave Cockrum created the design before joining Marvel; the character found his place among the international team in Giant-Size X-Men #1. His faith, humor and compassion are central to many portrayals.

Creators // team debut

Len Wein (writer) and Dave Cockrum (artist and character designer). Nightcrawler joined the new team in Giant-Size X-Men #1.

Powers & abilities

Short-range teleportation, exceptional agility and a prehensile tail. Teleportation leaves a cloud of sulfurous smoke and usually requires him to know or see his destination.

Margin note

Cockrum first designed Nightcrawler for a different comic-book setting. After that project did not use the character, he brought the design with him to Marvel's new X-Men.

Start reading

  • Giant-Size X-Men #1 (1975)
  • “Days of Future Past,” The Uncanny X-Men #141–142 (1981)
  • Excalibur #1 (1988)

FILE 11 // NO SKIN CONTACT

X-Man // Southern belle

Rogue

Anna Marie · full birth name unrevealed

First appearance Avengers Annual #10 · 1981

The hardest power to live with may be the one that keeps you apart.— Field archive

Origin // Field notes

Rogue's mutant power emerged as a teenager: skin contact can absorb another person's memories, abilities and life energy. After an early encounter left Carol Danvers powerless for a time, Rogue sought help from the X-Men and joined the team. Her family name has deliberately remained unrevealed in mainstream comics.

Creators // debut

Chris Claremont (writer) and Michael Golden (artist). Rogue's first appearance was in Avengers Annual #10.

Powers & abilities

Absorbs memories, skills and powers through touch, usually temporarily, while the other person is weakened. Prolonged contact can be dangerous. Her long-running access to some of Carol Danvers' abilities has varied by era.

Margin note

Rogue's first appearance was not in an X-Men issue: she debuted as an adversary in Avengers Annual #10 before joining the X-Men.

Start reading

  • Avengers Annual #10 (1981)
  • The Uncanny X-Men #171 (1983)
  • Rogue & Gambit #1–5 (2018)
